# Medical-source discipline

1. Read `course/COURSE.md` and the relevant `course/processed/*.md` file. Imports are searchable caches; check original files when figures/tables, headings or text extraction are ambiguous.
2. Cite course statements as `(LectureName.pdf, p. 4)`, `(LectureName.pptx, slide 7)`, or `(Handout.docx, paragraph 12)`. Only use anchors actually present. Never invent references.
3. Record provenance of each nontrivial claim:
   - **COURSE NOTE**: lecturer-provided material and exact location. Sets learning/exam scope.
   - **EXTERNAL VERIFIED**: checked textbook/guideline/paper, with title, date/edition and URL when available.
   - **TEACHING EXAMPLE**: illustrative analogy, hypothetical vignette or simplified drawing.
4. If the course conflicts with current evidence, state both versions, their sources, and why it matters; do not silently rewrite the professor's content. For fast-changing guidelines specify publication date/jurisdiction or say you cannot verify.
5. Do not use model memory as proof or invent exact page numbers, journal studies, contraindications or numeric ranges.
6. Do not upload sensitive patient information. Respect course-handout and third-party rights; avoid auto-publishing professor notes to the public GitHub repository.
